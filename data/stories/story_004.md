## Story for Chunk 004 - Night Passage

The Night Passage left Lydon under a rainless black sky, its corridor lamps the only **safeguard** against the dark panes. Maren Chen occupied a second-class compartment with one suitcase and the **pessimism** of a man who had learned that departures rarely improve a life. Across from him sat Orin Vale, a **curmudgeon** in an expensive coat whose **luxury** watch caught the light whenever the train lurched. Vale tried to **inveigle** Maren into sharing the lower berth, saying the conductor had made a **blatant** error and might **extend** the inconvenience all night, though a locked door would **preclude** it. Maren declined. Between them lay a **cistern** of silence, deepened by the **spectrum** of accents drifting from the **cosmopolitan** carriage. Vale opened a thin novel and read its **preface** aloud in a **grandiloquent** voice. The **inimitable** performance had an **incongruity** that made Maren look up. "You travel under your own name?" Vale asked. Maren said he did. "Then you are either brave or **feckless**." The remark let a small suspicion **burgeon** in him. Vale's severe **teetotalism**, the untouched flask in his pocket, and the way he watched every passing attendant suggested a man rehearsing someone else's habits.

夜行列车从莱登驶出，天空没有雨，只有漆黑的夜色。走廊的灯光是车窗外黑暗中唯一的保护。马伦·陈坐在二等车厢里，带着一只手提箱和对离别的悲观。他对面坐着奥林·韦尔，一个穿昂贵外套的坏脾气老人，火车摇晃时，他的名表不断反光。韦尔哄马伦共用下铺，说列车员犯了明显错误，麻烦可能会持续整夜。马伦拒绝了。两人之间沉默得像一口深井，多国旅客的口音从车厢里飘来。韦尔朗读小说序言，腔调浮夸，显得很不协调。马伦因此抬头。韦尔问他是否用真名旅行，马伦回答是。韦尔说，这意味着他不是勇敢就是无能。马伦心里生出疑心。韦尔严厉戒酒，口袋里的酒壶却始终未动，又不断留意路过的乘务员，像是在排练另一个人的习惯。

At midnight, a stranger in a porter’s grey coat stopped at their door and asked Maren to **sequester** himself in the washroom for a minute. His request seemed to **exasperate** Vale, who called the stranger a **pariah** and claimed the railway would **authorize** no such intrusion. But the man produced two leather wallets and made a **proffer**: names exchanged, tickets exchanged, no questions asked. He said a loan at **usury** rates had left him in **penury**, and that a **dingy** office in the capital had made him carry a name that was not his. Vale became **censorious**. "Do not **utter** another lie," he said. The stranger, **restless** but not pleading, replied, "I am **loath** to die as Orin Vale." His **aversion** to a **partial** identity was **punctuated** by the wheels, and he said Vale meant to **embezzle** his remaining life. His papers were **polished**, his forged rail pass ready for him to **debark** at the frontier. Maren realized the stranger had chosen Vale's name before ever entering their compartment. "This is no **audition**," Maren said. "Why should I make you **secure**?" The stranger answered that he did not want Maren to **palliate** a crime, only to decide which face deserved the checkpoint. Vale's **vitality** drained into a **lethargic** stillness. He could not **elucidate** why his passport showed Maren's photograph, though he had been so **subservient** to the stranger's directions moments before.

午夜时，一个穿灰色行李员外套的陌生人停在门口，要马伦到盥洗室单独待一分钟。这个要求似乎惹恼了韦尔，他把陌生人叫作弃民，并说铁路不会准许这种闯入。然而陌生人拿出两只皮夹，提出交换姓名和车票，不问缘由。他说一笔按高利率计息的贷款使他陷入贫困，首都一间昏暗的办公室逼他背负不属于自己的名字。韦尔严厉地叫他别再说谎。陌生人焦躁却并未乞求，说自己不愿以奥林·韦尔的身份死去。车轮声打断了他对残缺身份的厌恶，他说韦尔想吞没他余下的人生。他的证件制作精良，还有一张伪造的铁路通行证，准备在边境下车。马伦意识到，陌生人在进车厢前就选中了韦尔的名字。“这不是试演，”马伦说，“我为什么要让你脱身。”陌生人回答，他不想让马伦粉饰罪行，只想让他决定哪个面孔该被送到检查站。韦尔的活力消失，变得迟钝。他无法解释为何自己的护照上是马伦的照片，尽管片刻前他还顺从地听从陌生人的安排。

The train entered a mountain tunnel, and the three men began to **interrogate** one another beneath a **taut** wire that held the yellow lamp steady. Vale said the stranger was a **bigot** who hated him for his wealth; the stranger answered that Vale's **abjection** was voluntary, a retreat into money whenever **humility** was required. Maren felt a **misgiving** sharpen. He had once delivered people to authorities because procedure seemed less dangerous than judgment, and that habit was the **bane** of his life. The stranger said Vale had agreed to an identity swap so he could **vitiate** an old record and vanish. Vale gave a **shrug**, then offered to **reconnoiter** the corridor for police. "Don't **meddle**," he said, addressing Maren, "you belong in a **conservatory**, trimming dead leaves." The insult carried a familiar **stigma**, but Maren saw the **bound** hands under Vale's coat and a **buck** knife tucked near the **banister**. The stranger's **ancillary** account lacked **comity**, yet it was no **oration**. The stranger used a hidden cuff key to **revive** feeling in one wrist; a line of **viscous** blood marked the cuff. He spoke in the precise **terminology** of border warrants. "I was meant to **recycle** Vale's life for **pelf**," he said. "He calls me a **churl**, then hires me to become him." Vale's face turned toward the glass. "You want a **revolt** against names," he said. "I am **spent** of it."

列车驶入山中隧道，三人在一根拉紧、固定着昏黄吊灯的电线下互相盘问。韦尔说陌生人是仇富的偏执者。陌生人回答，韦尔的落魄是自愿的，每当需要谦卑时，他就躲进金钱里。马伦的不安越来越强烈。他曾因程序比判断看起来更安全，便把人交给当局，这个习惯一直是他人生的祸根。陌生人说，韦尔同意交换身份，是为了毁掉旧记录并消失。韦尔耸耸肩，提出去走廊侦察警察，还叫马伦别多管闲事，说他该待在温室里修剪枯叶。侮辱勾起马伦熟悉的污名感，但他看见韦尔外套下被束缚的双手，以及靠近扶手的一把折刀。陌生人的补充说法并不友善，却也不是一篇演说。陌生人用藏着的手铐钥匙让一只手腕恢复知觉，黏稠的血在手铐上留下一线痕迹。他用边境通缉令的精确术语说，自己本该为了钱把韦尔的人生重新利用。韦尔叫他粗人，却雇他变成自己。韦尔望着车窗，说他们是想反抗姓名，而他已经对此精疲力竭。

When the train cleared the tunnel, Vale lunged across the compartment and tried to **belabor** the stranger with the flask. Maren caught his arm. The blow was no **sacrilege**, but terror, and the stranger's **pithy** warning, "He carries the warrant," stopped Maren from releasing him. The stranger confessed that he had led an armed **incursion** years earlier, then tried to **indurate** his conscience by wearing borrowed identities. Vale had been his former handler, not his victim. A notice in the stranger's wallet showed a **herpetologist** among the dead, a detail so small it made Maren want to **submerge** the whole story in silence. The **incandescent** lamps flickered as the border **signal** appeared ahead. Vale began to **palter**, claiming he had only helped the man escape. He gestured with his chin toward the emergency bell, as though violence were a **parenthesis** in an otherwise respectable life. "You **brag** about loyalty," the stranger said, "yet your record is **sterile** of any act that proves it." From the train's little **pantry**, Maren brought water. Vale tried to **denigrate** the stranger, blaming his **narcissism**, but Maren saw the checkpoint **impend** through the snow. There was no time to dwell on the past. His **discretion**, long mistaken for **passivity**, told him that neither man's story was **apropos** to the question facing him.

列车驶出隧道后，韦尔扑过来，想用酒壶猛击陌生人。马伦抓住他的手臂。那一击不是亵渎，而是出于恐惧；陌生人简洁地警告说通缉令在韦尔身上，这让马伦没有放手。陌生人承认自己多年前带领过一次武装侵入，后来借用别人的身份，想让良心变得冷硬。韦尔曾是他的联络人，不是受害者。陌生人的皮夹里有一则死亡名单，其中包括一名两栖爬行动物学家。这个细节如此微小，却让马伦想把整个故事沉入沉默。白炽灯闪烁，边境的信号出现在前方。韦尔开始含糊其词，说自己只是协助那人逃跑。他用下巴示意紧急铃，仿佛暴力只是体面人生的一段插曲。陌生人说韦尔夸耀忠诚，但他的记录里没有任何行为能证明这一点。马伦从小食品室拿来水。韦尔诋毁陌生人，归咎于他的自恋，马伦却看见检查站在雪中逼近。没有时间再反复纠缠过去。他曾误以为是被动的谨慎，此刻告诉他，两人的故事都不适合回答眼前的问题。

At the border stop, Maren used the brake cord to **kindle** the alarm and gave the conductor a plain account, with no **tinge** of mercy and no **immature** wish to become either man's judge. Vale tried to **shrink** behind the stranger, then called him a **miser** of truth. The stranger's **sinew** went rigid as he tried to **resist** the guards, but an **ornithology** book fell from his coat, exposing the original tickets. Maren did not **proclaim** either identity as **accidental**, nor **retouch** their accounts to make himself seem wiser. He asked the conductor to **adjourn** the formal handover until both men could be checked against the warrant, rather than let a **pollster** of rumor decide them on the platform. The delay caused an **upheaval** among passengers, not an **inferno**, and Maren took one **sip** of cold tea while the guards worked through the **dearth** of usable records. Vale grew **overbearing**, trying to **gobble** every answer, and accused Maren of **defamation** meant to **foment** suspicion. Maren told the officers to **scrutinize** the photographs and stay **levelheaded**. His brief **rapport** with the stranger did not excuse him; the man began to **reel**, asked to **abscond**, then tried to **quaff** from Vale's flask. Maren refused. He asked the guards to **revise** neither name, though Vale would **wag** a finger and invoke a **noble** past. The worn tickets seemed to **prefigure** the truth: both men had traded identities, each hoping to make the other carry the consequence. The officers, too **finicky** to accept an **elaborate** tale, did not let confusion **frustrate** them. Their question had the bite of **satire**: which prisoner had bought the ticket? When each man tried to **accede** to the other's account of blame, Maren recognized the **ineluctable** answer. He would **stipulate**, in his formal declaration, that the guards deliver one prisoner, not two. He gave them the stranger, whose warrant and original ticket matched, and kept Vale for identification. Vale tried to **nettle** him, but Maren would not **wield** pity as an excuse. The stranger's **turpitude** had made him **founder**; his borrowed name was only a **husk**. A **boor** in a customs coat tried to **discourage** the delay, but Maren knew that speed was only one **aspect** of justice. As the train moved on, Maren found the decision **salutary**, though its comfort was **nil**. He turned the compartment light **off**, felt the cars **draw** away from the border, and knew that accepting **futility** was no longer his **default**. A **quail** cried somewhere beyond the platform, and he watched the frontier fade.

列车在边境停下时，马伦拉响制动绳，触发警报。他向列车员作了朴素说明，不带怜悯的色彩，也没有幼稚地想成为任何一人的法官。韦尔试图躲到陌生人身后，又说陌生人吝惜真相。陌生人在警卫面前抵抗，肌肉绷紧，一本鸟类学书从外套里掉出，露出原始车票。马伦没有宣布任何一个身份是偶然的，也没有修饰故事来显得自己更聪明。他要求列车员推迟正式交接，先把两人同通缉令核对，而不是让站台上的流言决定他们。延迟在乘客中引起骚动，却没有变成烈火般的混乱。马伦喝了一小口冷茶，警卫面对可用记录的缺乏继续查验。韦尔蛮横地抢着回答，指控马伦诽谤并煽动怀疑。马伦让警员仔细审看照片，保持冷静。他与陌生人短暂建立的理解并不能免除陌生人的责任。陌生人摇晃着想逃，又要大口喝韦尔酒壶里的酒，马伦拒绝了。他请警卫不要修改任一姓名，尽管韦尔不断指着人，并搬出高贵的过去。破旧车票预示了真相，两人都交换过身份，都希望对方承担后果。警员虽对细节挑剔，却没有被复杂说辞挫败。他们讽刺地问，究竟是谁买了车票。两人都试图把责任归咎于对方时，马伦明白答案无法逃避。他在正式声明中规定，警卫只能移交一人，不能交出两人。他把通缉令和原始车票都吻合的陌生人交给警卫，同时留下韦尔等待核验。韦尔想激怒他，马伦却不把怜悯当作借口来使用。陌生人的卑劣行径使他垮掉，借来的姓名只是一副空壳。一名身穿海关制服的粗鲁家伙试图劝阻这场延误，但马伦知道，速度只是正义的一个方面。列车再次开动，马伦觉得这个决定有益，尽管安慰几乎为零。他关掉车厢灯，感到列车远离边境，明白接受徒劳不再是他的默认选择。站台外不知何处传来鹌鹑的叫声，他看着边境渐渐消失。
