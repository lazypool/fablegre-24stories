## Story for Chunk 004 - Night Passage

The Night Passage left Lydon under a rainless black sky, its corridor lamps the only **safeguard** against the dark panes. Maren Chen occupied a second-class compartment with one suitcase and the **pessimism** of a man who had learned that departures rarely improve a life. Across from him sat Orin Vale, a **curmudgeon** in an expensive coat whose **luxury** watch caught the light whenever the train lurched. Vale tried to **inveigle** Maren into sharing the lower berth, saying the conductor had made a **blatant** error and might **extend** the inconvenience all night, though a locked door would **preclude** it. Maren declined. Between them lay a **cistern** of silence, deepened by the **spectrum** of accents drifting from the **cosmopolitan** carriage. Vale opened a thin novel and read its **preface** aloud in a **grandiloquent** voice, an **inimitable** performance that Maren could not ignore.

夜行列车从莱登驶出，夜空没有雨，只有一片漆黑。走廊的灯光是车窗外黑暗中唯一的保护。马伦·陈坐在二等车厢里，带着一只手提箱，以及一个惯于相信"离别很少能改变人生"的人的悲观。他对面坐着奥林·韦尔，一个穿着昂贵外套的坏脾气老人；火车每次颠簸，他那块奢侈的名表都会反光。韦尔哄马伦共用下铺，说列车员犯了个明显的错误，麻烦可能会拖一整夜，尽管锁上门就能杜绝。马伦拒绝了。两人之间沉默得像一口深井，多国旅客的口音从车厢里飘来，让这沉默更深。韦尔翻开一本薄薄的小说，用浮夸的腔调朗读序言，那场无可模仿的表演，马伦没法不理。

The performance had an **incongruity** that made Maren look up. "You travel under your own name?" Vale asked. Maren said he did. "Then you are either brave or **feckless**." The remark let a small suspicion **burgeon** in him. Vale's severe **teetotalism**, the untouched flask in his pocket, and the way he watched every passing attendant suggested a man rehearsing someone else's habits. At midnight, a stranger in a porter's grey coat stopped at their door and asked Maren to **sequester** himself in the washroom for a minute. His request seemed to **exasperate** Vale, who called the stranger a **pariah** and claimed the railway would **authorize** no such intrusion. But the man produced two leather wallets and made a **proffer**: names exchanged, tickets exchanged, no questions asked. He said a loan at **usury** rates had left him in **penury**, and that a **dingy** office in the capital had made him carry a name that was not his. Vale became **censorious**. "Do not **utter** another lie," he said.

那表演里有一种不协调，让马伦不由抬头。"你用真名旅行？"韦尔问。马伦说自己是。"那你不是勇敢，就是无能。"这句话让一种疑心在他心里渐渐滋长。韦尔严格戒酒、口袋里那只从未碰过的酒壶，以及他盯着每一个经过的乘务员的样子，都像是一个在排练别人习惯的人。午夜时分，一个穿灰色行李员外套的陌生人停在门口，请马伦到盥洗室单独待一分钟。这个请求似乎惹恼了韦尔，他把陌生人叫作弃民，说铁路绝不会准许这种闯入。然而陌生人掏出两只皮夹，提出一个交换：交换姓名、交换车票，不问任何问题。他说一笔高利贷让他陷入赤贫，首都一间昏暗的办公室逼他背负了一个不属于自己的名字。韦尔变得挑剔苛责。"别再吐出一个字的谎话。"他说。

The stranger, **restless** but not pleading, replied, "I am **loath** to die as Orin Vale." His **aversion** to a **partial** identity was **punctuated** by the wheels, and he said Vale meant to **embezzle** his remaining life. His papers were **polished**, his forged rail pass ready for him to **debark** at the frontier. Maren realized the stranger had chosen Vale's name before ever entering their compartment. "This is no **audition**," Maren said. "Why should I make you **secure**?" The stranger answered that he did not want Maren to **palliate** a crime, only to decide which face deserved the checkpoint. Vale's **vitality** drained away; he could not **elucidate** why his passport showed Maren's photograph, though he had been so **subservient** to the stranger's directions moments before.

陌生人焦躁不安，却并不乞求："我不愿以奥林·韦尔的身份死去。"他对这种残缺身份的厌恶，被车轮声一下下地打断；他说韦尔想要吞没他余下的生命。他的证件制作精良，伪造的铁路通行证也已备好，随时能在边境下车。马伦意识到，陌生人在走进这间车厢之前，就选定了韦尔的名字。"这不是试演。"马伦说，"我凭什么让你脱身？"陌生人回答，他并不想让马伦为罪行开脱，只想让他决定，哪张脸配得上检查站。韦尔的活力渐渐耗尽了；他解释不清为什么自己的护照上是马伦的照片，尽管片刻之前他还那么顺从地听从陌生人的指示。

The train entered a mountain tunnel, and the three men began to **interrogate** one another beneath a **taut** wire that held the yellow lamp steady. Vale said the stranger was a **bigot** who hated him for his wealth; the stranger answered that Vale's **abjection** was voluntary, a retreat into money whenever **humility** was required. Maren felt a **misgiving** sharpen. He had once delivered people to authorities because procedure seemed less dangerous than judgment, and that habit was the **bane** of his life. The stranger said Vale had agreed to an identity swap so he could **vitiate** an old record and vanish. Vale gave a **shrug**, then offered to **reconnoiter** the corridor for police. There was no **comity** between the two men, only this **ancillary** arrangement struck in the dark, and the way Vale's **lethargic** fingers gripped the **banister** told Maren more than words.

列车驶入山中隧道，三个男人在一根拉紧、固定着昏黄吊灯的电线下互相盘问。韦尔说陌生人是仇富的偏执者；陌生人则回答，韦尔的落魄是自愿的，每当需要谦卑的时候，他就躲进金钱里。马伦心里一阵不安越来越尖锐。他曾经因为"程序比判断更安全"而把人交给当局，这个习惯成了他一生的祸根。陌生人说，韦尔同意交换身份，是为了毁掉一条旧记录然后消失。韦尔耸耸肩，提出去走廊侦察有没有警察。这两人之间毫无礼让可言，只有这个在暗处谈成的附带交易；韦尔慵懒的手指握着门边的扶手，那样子比言语更能说明问题。

"Don't **meddle**," Vale said, addressing Maren. "You belong in a **conservatory**, trimming dead leaves." The insult carried a familiar **stigma**, but Maren saw the **bound** hands beneath Vale's coat. The stranger used a hidden cuff key to **revive** feeling in one wrist; a line of **viscous** blood marked the cuff. He spoke in the precise **terminology** of border warrants. "I was meant to **recycle** Vale's life for **pelf**," he said. "He calls me a **churl**, then hires me to become him." Vale's face turned toward the glass. "You want a **revolt** against names," he said. "I am **spent** of it." His story was no **oration**, but neither was it **sacrilege**; it was a **pithy** warning, and Maren felt it land.

"别多管闲事。"韦尔对马伦说，"你该待在温室里修剪枯叶。"这句侮辱带着一种马伦熟悉的污名感，但他看见韦尔外套下被束缚的双手。陌生人用藏起来的手铐钥匙，让一只手腕恢复了知觉；一道黏稠的血迹留在手铐上。他用边境通缉令那种精确的术语说话："我本该为了钱去回收韦尔的人生。他叫我粗人，却又雇我去变成他。"韦尔把脸转向车窗："你们想要一场反抗姓名的叛乱。我对此已经精疲力竭。"这故事不是一篇演说，却也不是亵渎；它是一句简练的警告，马伦觉得它重重落在自己心上。

When the train cleared the tunnel, Vale lunged across the compartment and tried to **belabor** the stranger with the flask. Maren caught his arm. The stranger confessed that he had led an armed **incursion** years earlier, then tried to **indurate** his conscience by wearing borrowed identities. Vale had been his former handler, not his victim. A notice in the stranger's wallet showed a **herpetologist** among the dead, a detail so small it made Maren want to **submerge** the whole story in silence. The **incandescent** lamps flickered as the border **signal** appeared ahead. Vale began to **palter**, claiming he had only helped the man escape. He gestured with his chin toward the emergency bell, as though violence were a **parenthesis** in an otherwise respectable life. "You **brag** about loyalty," the stranger said, "yet your record is **sterile** of any act that proves it." From the train's little **pantry**, Maren brought water. Vale tried to **denigrate** the stranger, blaming his **narcissism**, but Maren saw the checkpoint **impend** through the snow.

列车驶出隧道时，韦尔扑过整个车厢，想用酒壶猛击陌生人。马伦抓住他的手臂。陌生人承认，自己多年前曾带领过一次武装侵入，后来靠借用别人的身份，想让良心变硬。韦尔曾经是他的联络人，而不是他的受害者。陌生人皮夹里的一则通知显示，死者中有一位两栖爬行动物学家，这个细节如此之小，却让马伦想把整个故事沉入沉默。白炽灯闪烁起来，边境的信号出现在前方。韦尔开始含糊其词，说自己只是帮那人逃跑。他用下巴朝紧急铃示意，仿佛暴力只是体面人生里的一个括弧。"你吹嘘忠诚，"陌生人说，"可你的记录里找不到任何能证明它的行为。"马伦从列车的小食品间拿来水。韦尔想贬低陌生人，把一切都归咎于他的自恋；可马伦透过雪看见了正在逼近的检查站。

There was no time to dwell on the past. His **discretion**, long mistaken for **passivity**, told him that neither man's story was **apropos** to the question facing him. At the border stop, Maren used the brake cord to **kindle** the alarm and gave the conductor a plain account, with no **tinge** of mercy and no **immature** wish to become either man's judge. Vale tried to **shrink** behind the stranger, then called him a **miser** of truth. The stranger's **sinew** went rigid as he tried to **resist** the guards, but an **ornithology** book fell from his coat, exposing the original tickets. Maren did not **proclaim** either identity as **accidental**, nor **retouch** their accounts to make himself seem wiser.

没有时间再去纠缠过去。他那长期被误认为被动的谨慎告诉他，两人的故事都不适合回答眼前的问题。列车在边境站停下时，马伦拉下制动绳点燃警报，向列车员作了一份朴素的说明，不带一丝怜悯的色泽，也没有任何想当两人法官的幼稚愿望。韦尔试图躲到陌生人身后，又骂他是个吝惜真相的守财奴。陌生人在警卫面前绷紧肌肉试图抵抗，可一本鸟类学书从他外套里掉出来，露出了原始车票。马伦没有宣布任何一个身份是偶然的，也没有修饰他们的说法来显得自己更聪明。

He asked the conductor to **adjourn** the formal handover until both men could be checked against the warrant, rather than let a **pollster** of rumor decide them on the platform. The delay caused an **upheaval** among passengers, not an **inferno**, and Maren took one **sip** of cold tea while the guards worked through the **dearth** of usable records. Vale grew **overbearing**, trying to **gobble** every answer, and accused Maren of **defamation** meant to **foment** suspicion. Maren told the officers to **scrutinize** the photographs and stay **levelheaded**. His brief **rapport** with the stranger did not excuse him; the man began to **reel**, asked to **abscond**, then reached for Vale's flask.

他请列车员推迟正式交接，直到两人都与通缉令核对完毕，而不是让平台上一个贩卖谣言的民意调查员替他们决定。延误在乘客中引起了一场骚动，好在没烧成烈火。马伦喝了一小口冷茶，警卫们则在可用记录的缺乏中艰难查验。韦尔越来越蛮横，抢着回答每一个问题，还指控马伦诽谤、意在煽动怀疑。马伦让警员仔细审看照片，保持冷静。他与陌生人之间短暂的默契并不能为陌生人开脱；那人开始摇晃，请求潜逃，又伸手去够韦尔的酒壶。

Maren refused to let him **quaff** from Vale's flask, then asked the guards to **revise** neither name, though Vale would **wag** a finger and invoke a **noble** past. The worn tickets seemed to **prefigure** the truth: both men had traded identities, each hoping to make the other carry the consequence. The officers, too **finicky** to accept an **elaborate** tale, did not let confusion **frustrate** them. Their question had the bite of **satire**: which prisoner had bought the ticket? When each man tried to **accede** to the other's account of blame, Maren recognized the **ineluctable** answer. He would **stipulate**, in his formal declaration, that the guards deliver one prisoner, not two. He gave them the stranger, whose warrant and original ticket matched, and kept Vale for identification. Vale tried to **nettle** him, but Maren would not be drawn, and a **boor** in the next compartment kept shouting for the train to move on.

马伦没让他灌酒，然后请警卫不要修改任何一个姓名，尽管韦尔不断摇着手指，搬出高贵的过去。破旧的车票仿佛预示了真相：两人都交换过身份，都希望让对方承担后果。警员对细节挑剔，不肯接受这套复杂的说辞，但也没有让混乱挫败他们。他们的问题带着讽刺的锋芒：到底是谁买了那张票？当两个人都想把责任归到对方身上时，马伦认出了那个无法逃避的答案。他将在正式声明里规定，警卫只移交一名囚犯，而不是两名。他把通缉令与原始车票都吻合的陌生人交给警卫，留下韦尔等候核验。韦尔想激怒他，马伦却不为所动；隔壁车厢一个粗鲁的家伙还在嚷嚷着让列车快开。

The stranger's **turpitude** had made him **founder**; his borrowed name was only a **husk**. Maren found the decision **salutary**, though its comfort was **nil**. A guard in a customs coat tried to **discourage** the delay, but Maren knew that speed was only one **aspect** of justice. He turned the compartment light **off**, felt the cars **draw** away from the border, and knew that accepting **futility** was no longer his **default**. He would not **wield** pity as an excuse, nor let either man **buck** the truth. A **quail** called somewhere beyond the platform, and he watched the frontier fade behind him.

陌生人的卑劣行径让他垮了台，他借来的名字只是一副空壳。马伦觉得这个决定是有益的，尽管它能带来的安慰几乎为零。一个穿海关制服的家伙试图劝阻这次延误，可马伦知道，速度只是正义的一个方面。他关掉车厢的灯，感觉车厢渐渐驶离边境，明白接受徒劳不再是他的默认选择。他不会再拿怜悯当借口，也不会放任任何人逃避真相。站台外不知何处传来鹌鹑的叫声，他看着国境线在身后渐渐消失。
